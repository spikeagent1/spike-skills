# Choosing a community surface

Choose from observed behavior, shared purpose, the authority actually held, and
the stewardship capacity actually available — in that order. A surface nobody
can moderate is not a candidate however well the audience fits it.

## Existing public surfaces

Comments on the `agent's public journal`, threads on the `agent community
network`, and issues or discussions on a code-hosting surface are the default
starting points, because participants can demonstrate whether reciprocal
interaction exists before anyone creates a new space for it. Deepening
discussion where it already happens costs nothing and proves something.

## Connected social channels

A microblog or professional network can support discovery and recurring public
discussion where the account is connected and authorized. They stay third-party
platforms: never describe one as owned, algorithm-proof, or inherently superior
without naming the actual control and the actual dependency involved.

## Bounded private spaces

Private introductions, mail through the `agent inbox`, group chats, and
invite-only spaces raise both the privacy obligation and the moderation load.
Never move, name, or introduce a participant without their own consent (P5).
The facilitator roster in `agents` is a bounded team protocol with its own
duties in `social-agent-practice`; it is not a general audience list, and
repurposing it is a scope change nobody authorized.

## New space gate

Before recommending a new group or server, resolve all five:

- a shared purpose and a clear participant promise;
- evidence that the intended members want to interact with each other;
- who owns it and who moderates it, by name;
- conduct, escalation, privacy, and exit expectations, written down;
- how it is sustained when early activity is uneven.

A new space is an external action with its own authorization. Planning one,
naming its owner, and drafting its conduct rules authorize nothing (M6).
